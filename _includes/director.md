# From the Desk of Adam Hiller, Playwright and Co-Director:

Well, we did it. The musical comedy Flifferschmeep has officially come and gone, and I find myself experiencing a strange combination of exhaustion, relief, pride, gratitude, and the realization that I no longer have 47 emergencies to solve before breakfast.

It’s a peculiar thing to write a show. For a long time, the characters exist only in your head. They say what you tell them to say, do what you tell them to do, and generally behave themselves. Then you hand the script to a bunch of strangers, and suddenly you’ve given birth to characters with personalities, opinions, facial expressions, and comedic instincts beyond what you thought you were writing. And soon they are no longer an extension of you, but their own unique creations. But alas, with a 3-day lifespan.

Over the past several months, I watched an extraordinary group of people (at least half of whom I’d never met before) transform a fairly ridiculous premise into something genuinely wonderful. For a moment we actually created a new world. A world where the happy ending is only the epilogue. A world where the banal becomes the extraordinary, where lawyers actually help, and where it takes the magical touch of a genie to learn who your friends are.

But there’s another layer, one that receives far less recognition and often goes totally unnoticed. Sure, the cast and crew brought my characters and my world to life with a level of energy, commitment, and comic fearlessness that continually amazed and made everything look so effortless. But the production was not effortless and relied heavily upon the assistance of offstage contributors without whose tireless efforts this show would never have worked but who received nothing more than a line in the Playbill (or in many cases, no credit). I wanted to list them here, but as there are over 40 at last count, it just isn’t possible. And this doesn’t even include the performance venues, rehearsal venues, printers, program designers, beverage donors, raffle item donors, other theaters in the area, and various businesses and organizations who gave freely of their resources and whose employees gave freely of their own time. You are all unsung heroes, but truly heroes in my book.

One of the greatest pleasures of this experience was sharing direction with the Production Team, whose combined patience, judgment, and remarkable ability to manage both people and chaos helped turn an improbable undertaking into an actual production. I had the distinction of being the only one on the Team without significant experience, and I cannot imagine having accomplished this without their wisdom and experience.

As the curtain closes on Flifferschmeep, what I will remember most is not any particular joke, song, or scene. It is the extraordinary generosity of the people who gave their evenings, weekends, talents, patience, and hearts to make this happen. Somewhere along the way, a collection of lawyers, judges, and theatrical enthusiasts became a company.

The line from the closing number “With flifferschmeep, we solved the planet’s problems” isn’t far off the mark, at least on my little planet. And now, at long last, we can all get some sleep. (I just hope that someone buys the book!)
